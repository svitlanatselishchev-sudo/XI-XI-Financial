# XI:XI brand and content quality guide

## Visual identity

The primary identity is the explicit **XI:XI** wordmark. The gold colon is the alignment point between the two `XI` groups. The compact favicon uses the same readable `XI:XI` lettering rather than an abstract BX-style monogram.

Core colors:

- Ink `#171717` for primary text and dark surfaces
- Paper `#F7F4EF` for the warm page background
- White `#FFFFFF` for cards and form surfaces
- Brass `#B38D5A` for accents and calls to action
- Brass dark `#80633D` for accent text on light backgrounds
- Body text `#4D4740` or darker on light backgrounds

Never use light gold, muted gray, or white text directly on a similarly light surface. Text must have a clear contrast boundary through a dark surface, white card, border, or stronger text color.

## Content and accounting terminology

Use these terms consistently:

- **General ledger**, not “ledger” when referring to the complete accounting record
- **Bank reconciliation**, not “bank match,” when comparing the ledger with a bank statement
- **Accounts receivable** for customer amounts owed to the business
- **Accounts payable** for amounts the business owes vendors
- **Intercompany receivable/payable** for balances between related entities
- **Sales tax collected and sales tax remitted** for tax charged to customers versus paid to the tax authority
- **Use tax** for taxable purchases where sales tax was not charged, subject to the applicable jurisdiction and facts
- **Point-of-sale (POS) report** on first use, then POS
- **Chart of accounts** without a hyphen
- **Month-end close** for the period-end close process
- **Uncleared or outstanding checks** depending on whether the accounting system or bank-reconciliation context is intended

Content should distinguish operational reconciliation support from tax, legal, or attest services. Use “review,” “reconciliation,” “verification,” and “preparation support” unless the firm is explicitly licensed and engaged to provide tax advice, legal advice, audit, or assurance.

## Editorial rules

- Prefer plain language over jargon, then explain necessary accounting terms.
- Use sentence case for headings and buttons.
- Use an em dash consistently, with spaces around it.
- Avoid absolute claims such as “every audit” or “always” unless supported by a defined scope.
- When discussing state tax, include a reminder that rules and filing obligations depend on current law, jurisdiction, registration, and the taxpayer’s facts.
