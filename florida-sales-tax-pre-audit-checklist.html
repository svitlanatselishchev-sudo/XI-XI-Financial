What the Florida Dept. of Revenue Looks For First: A Pre-Audit Checklist

Key Takeaway: Auditors start with the same few red flags every time — mismatched exemption codes and rounding drift top the list.

The Florida Department of Revenue doesn't examine every line of every return before deciding who gets a closer look. In practice, a small number of red flags account for most audit selections and most of the adjustments that come out of them. Knowing what those are lets you check your own filings before the state does.

1. A gap between sales tax collected and sales tax remitted

Point-of-sale systems calculate and collect tax at the register in real time. The return that eventually gets filed is a separate, manual (or semi-manual) step. Any difference between what the register says was collected and what the return says was remitted is one of the first things an examiner reconciles.

2. Missing or expired exemption and resale certificates

Every tax-exempt sale needs a valid certificate on file to support it. A business that sells exempt on paper but can't produce the certificate when asked will have that sale reclassified as taxable — plus penalties and interest on the difference.

3. Use tax on untaxed out-of-state purchases

When a business buys equipment or supplies from an out-of-state vendor that didn't charge Florida sales tax, Florida use tax is generally still owed on that purchase. This is one of the most commonly missed line items on a self-prepared return.

4. Rounding and rate-table drift across counties

Florida's state sales tax rate is layered with county surtaxes that vary by location. A business operating in more than one county — or using a POS system with an outdated rate table — can end up with small, consistent rounding differences that add up over a filing period.

5. Inconsistency between POS reports and the filed return

Examiners frequently start by asking for the point-of-sale system's sales summary and comparing it directly to the numbers on file. Any recurring gap between the two — even a small one — tends to be the first thread they pull.

A pre-filing self-check

  • Total your POS-reported taxable sales for the period and compare them directly to the return you filed.
  • Pull every exemption certificate on file and confirm none have expired.
  • Review out-of-state vendor invoices for untaxed purchases that may owe Florida use tax.
  • Confirm your POS system's tax rate table matches the current county-by-county rates.
  • Reconcile at least one full quarter, line by line, between the register and the filed return.

None of this replaces advice from a licensed tax professional on how to file or what to claim — that's a conversation worth having with your CPA or tax attorney.
